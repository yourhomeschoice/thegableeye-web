Air fryers compared: seven basket-style models for different kitchens

A practical guide to basket layout, cooking zones, controls and the details worth checking before you choose.

Our buying scenario

A basket-style air fryer can make sense for a quick meal, but the right layout depends on what you cook. A single basket keeps the arrangement simple; two baskets let you separate foods; and a removable divider can turn one cooking space into two. We compared seven selected models on their reported configurations, capacities, cooking modes, controls and cleaning requirements—not on measured cooking results.

THE GABLE’S VERDICT

For this seven-model shortlist, the Ninja DZ071 remains our provisional pick for its reported ability to switch between one 7-quart zone and two 3.5-quart zones. The Ninja DZ201C is the alternative we would examine first for two permanently separate baskets. The SL401 offers the largest stated total capacity, while the COSORI is a useful single-basket comparison for clearly listed controls. None of these positions establishes better cooking results, greater reliability or superiority across the wider market. Exact-model specifications should be checked before publication or purchase.

Why the configuration matters

Total quarts do not tell the whole story. The DZ071 offers a divided-or-combined cooking space, whereas the DZ201C and stacked Ninja models have two separate baskets. A larger stated capacity may help with bigger batches, but basket shape and usable arrangement still matter. Cooking modes, synchronization features, temperature controls and cleaning instructions can be more relevant to daily use than a long list of presets.

The air fryers in more detail

Ninja DZ071 — our provisional first choice

The DZ071 stands out because the reported removable divider gives you two ways to arrange the same cooking space: one 7-quart MegaZone or two 3.5-quart zones. Six modes, Smart Finish and Match Cook are listed, as are dishwasher-safe baskets and crisper plates. For someone alternating between one larger dish and two separate foods, that is a useful configuration to investigate. The full temperature range and physical dimensions are not settled, so check both before buying.

Ninja DZ201C — two independent baskets

The DZ201C is the clearest alternative if separate baskets are the point of the purchase. Its reported 8-quart total capacity is split between two 4-quart baskets, with Air Fry, Air Broil, Roast, Bake, Reheat and Dehydrate modes. A 105–450°F range and Smart Finish/Match Cook are also listed. Unlike the DZ071, the supplied information does not describe combining the baskets into one larger zone.

Ninja SL401 — the largest stated capacity

At a reported 10 quarts across two 5-quart baskets, the SL401 has the highest listed capacity in this group. Its stacked layout, removable meal racks, six modes and synchronization features offer another approach to preparing more than one item. But source candidates disagree on dimensions and dishwasher safety for baskets, plates and racks, and the temperature range is unverified. Those are important checks for a model whose physical arrangement is a key selling point.

Ninja SL201 — a stacked eight-quart alternative

The SL201 uses a stacked two-basket layout, with a reported 8-quart capacity split into two 4-quart baskets. Six modes, Smart Finish and Match Cook are listed. A 450°F maximum is reported, although the complete temperature range is not.

COSORI CAF-L501-KUS — detailed single-basket controls

For someone who wants one basket rather than multiple cooking zones, the 5-quart COSORI has a comparatively clear set of reported controls: 170–450°F, a 1–60 minute timer, seven named food presets, Preheat and Keep Warm. Its nonstick aluminum basket and crisper plate are described as dishwasher safe. A two-year warranty is listed. Its smaller stated capacity is a configuration difference, not evidence of poorer cooking.

Instant Pot Vortex 6QT — a straightforward single basket

This 6-quart Vortex is reported with Air Fry, Bake, Roast and Reheat modes and an adjustable 120–400°F range. Its basket is described as top-rack dishwasher safe. One source candidate also lists the removable tray as dishwasher safe. It offers one quart more stated capacity than the COSORI, but fewer named modes and no verified timer range.

Philips NA351/00R0 — a two-drawer option to verify

The Philips is described with one large and one small drawer, plus cooking-time synchronization. That asymmetrical arrangement may be worth examining if you want separate zones of different sizes. A two-year warranty is listed on US support or product pages.

What to check before choosing

Choose the basket arrangement first. Decide whether you need one basket, two independent baskets, a divided single zone or a stacked layout. Compare the shape and arrangement of the cooking space as well as the total quarts.

Check the controls on the exact model. The DZ201C and COSORI have reported temperature ranges, while the DZ071’s full range and the Instant Pot’s timer range remain unresolved. Do not infer that an undocumented control is absent.

Measure your counter and storage space. Check the exact unit’s width, depth, height and clearance needs.

Our conclusion

The Ninja DZ071 is our provisional first choice because its removable divider is reported to create either one 7-quart MegaZone or two 3.5-quart zones. That flexibility is the clearest distinction in this shortlist. The Ninja DZ201C is our principal alternative for someone who specifically wants two independent 4-quart baskets. Neither is a tested winner or a market-wide recommendation; the underlying specifications still require verification.

How we compared

We assessed the seven supplied candidates using reported basket configurations, capacity, cooking modes, controls, cleaning information and other practical details. The supplied research candidates were not independently source-verified. We did not perform hands-on cooking tests or measure performance, pricing, durability or reliability.
