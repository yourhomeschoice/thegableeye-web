Blenders compared: nine countertop models for different routines

A practical guide to pitcher sizes, personal cups, controls and the features worth checking before you buy.

Our buying scenario

A countertop blender can serve several different routines: larger batches, individual drinks, or recipes that call for particular settings. For this comparison, we looked at nine selected models and the features documented for each, rather than assuming that a bigger motor rating or a longer list of presets guarantees better results.

THE GABLE’S VERDICT

For these nine selected countertop blenders, the Vitamix A2500 remains The Gable’s pick for its combination of reported capacity, manual controls, named presets and warranty. The Ninja BN751 is the clearest alternative when switching between a full pitcher and personal cups matters more. KitchenAid, Breville and the remaining models provide different vessel and control combinations worth considering for particular routines. This is a feature-based editorial ordering, not a verified claim about blending performance or a ranking of the entire market.

Why the configuration matters

Container capacity is only part of the decision. A pitcher may suit shared drinks and larger recipes, while personal cups or a smaller blend-in jar offer a different serving format. Manual speeds and presets also serve different preferences: a preset describes an available control, not how well the machine handles its named task. Pay attention to usable liquid limits, especially for hot ingredients, and to the parts you will need to clean.

The blenders in more detail

Vitamix A2500 — The Gable’s first choice

The A2500 combines a reported 64-ounce low-profile container with variable speed, pulse and presets for smoothies, hot soups and frozen desserts. Dishwasher-safe status, a 2.2 peak horsepower rating and a 10-year full warranty are also listed. That is a broad documented feature set for this shortlist, but the horsepower number is a rating rather than a performance measurement.

Ninja BN751 — pitcher and personal cups

The BN751 gives you a 72-ounce pitcher, with a stated maximum liquid capacity of 64 ounces, and two 24-ounce single-serve cups. Low, Medium, High and Pulse controls sit alongside three Auto-iQ programs for smoothies, frozen drinks and nutrient extractions. Separate cup blades and a stacked pitcher blade are described; consult the exact cleaning instructions for safe handling.

KitchenAid KSB2070PL — glass jar and manual range

This KitchenAid has a reported 62-ounce glass jar, ten speeds, pulse and Smoothie, Ice Crushing and Clean presets. The supplied information describes asymmetrical blades. Its stated 40-ounce hot-liquid limit is lower than the jar’s total capacity and matters if you plan to blend warm ingredients.

Breville BBL620 — named programs and cleaning cycle

The Fresh & Furious has a 50-ounce jug, five manual speeds, an LCD display and Smoothie, Green Smoothie and Ice Crush programs, with Auto Clean also listed. The jug is described as dishwasher-safe and the lid components as top-rack safe.

Hamilton Beach 53602 — an extra travel jar

Alongside a 52-ounce glass jar, this model includes a reported 20-ounce blend-in jar and travel lid. Five speeds, pulse and three programs are listed, as are a twist-off blade collar and dishwasher-safe parts.

Hamilton Beach 53601 — familiar controls, one main jar

The 53601 has a 52-ounce glass jar, five speeds and pulse, with Smoothie, Ice Crush and Clean programs. Its jar and blade are described as dishwasher-safe. A 950-watt peak-power rating and three-year US limited warranty are reported.

Cuisinart BL-200 — glass jar and labeled controls

The BL-200 pairs a 48-ounce glass jar with five manual speeds and controls named Smoothie, Pulse, Milkshake, Cocktail (Coarse/Fine), Ice Crush and Clean. A removable blade assembly and three-year limited warranty are also reported.

Oster BLSTMEG-G00-000 — manual speed selection

The Oster offers a 48-ounce glass jar, eight speeds and a reported four-point stainless-steel blade. All parts other than the base are described as dishwasher-safe, and a three-year limited warranty is listed.

Vitamix ONE — a smaller, simpler format

The ONE has a reported 32-ounce BPA-free Tritan container, a single speed dial and a mini-tamper. Its listed dimensions are relatively compact within this group, and a two-year warranty is reported. It sits lower in this broad, feature-led shortlist because of its smaller vessel and narrower documented controls, not because its small-batch performance was tested or found lacking.

What to check before choosing

Match the vessel to your routine. Compare total jar size with stated usable liquid capacity, and consider whether personal cups or an additional travel jar would be genuinely useful. The Ninja BN751 and Hamilton Beach 53602 include extra serving formats; the KitchenAid has a separate reported limit for hot liquids.

Choose the controls you will use. Manual speeds, pulse and named presets are different ways to operate a blender. A smoothie or ice-crush label is not evidence of superior results, and electrical ratings are not directly comparable performance tests.

Check fit, cleaning and blade handling.

Verify the exact manufacturer instructions before washing removable blades or placing parts in a dishwasher.

Our conclusion

The Vitamix A2500 is our first choice on the strength of its reported 64-ounce container, variable speed and pulse, three named presets and 10-year full warranty. The Ninja BN751 is the strongest alternative if you want both a large pitcher and individual serving cups. These positions reflect the supplied specifications, not independently tested blending performance.

How we compared

We reviewed the nine supplied candidates for documented vessel formats, capacity, controls, presets, cleaning and blade information, plus electrical and warranty details where available. The underlying specifications were not independently verified, and we did not conduct hands-on blending tests or measure price, durability, reliability or results.
