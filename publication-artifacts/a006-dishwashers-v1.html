Dishwashers compared: 10 models, different priorities

An evidence-led look at quieter operation, rack flexibility and drying features — and the details worth checking before you buy.

Our buying scenario

A dishwasher has to work with your kitchen and your routine. Some households want a quieter machine for an open-plan space; others care more about fitting awkward glasses, pans and cutlery into each load. Drying options, wash programs and installation fit also deserve attention. We compared ten selected US models using the research information available to us, rather than treating a longer feature list as proof of better cleaning.

THE GABLE’S VERDICT

For this selected group, we pick the Bosch SHP78CM5N. Its reported balance of quieter operation, flexible loading and CrystalDry makes it the most coherent provisional choice on the information available. The JennAir JDPSS246LL deserves equal attention if the lowest listed noise figure is your priority.

Why the configuration matters

A third rack can make it easier to organize smaller items, but the rack design matters more than simply counting racks. Likewise, a quoted noise figure helps narrow a shortlist without telling you how a machine sounds in your own kitchen. And drying technologies have different names across brands; our research does not establish which one dries better in practice. Start with the exact model and your available space, then use the reported features to decide what is worth checking in detail.

How the ten models compare

Bosch SHP78CM5N - our first choice

The appeal of the Bosch is the combination of a reported 42 dBA rating, a flexible third rack and CrystalDry. Home Connect Wi-Fi is also listed. Heavy, Auto and Normal appear among its wash programs. Capacity, configuration and warranty information still warrant checking. This position reflects the documented mix of features, not a claim that Bosch cleans better than the others.

JennAir JDPSS246LL - the quieter listed alternative

At a reported 38 dBA, JennAir has the lowest listed noise figure of these ten models. Its stated 15-place capacity and three racks with dual rotating wash tubes give shoppers more to compare than the sound rating alone. The six named cycles include 1-Hour Wash, Cookware, Crystal, Normal, Refresh and Sensor.

LG LDT7808SS - flexible loading and a broad cycle list

LG is worth a closer look if rack adjustment and program choice matter to you. The available information describes 15 place settings, an adjustable upper rack, a height-adjustable third rack and ten programs, including Auto, Heavy, Delicate, Turbo and Express.

KitchenAid KDTM404KPS - space for varied loads

KitchenAid is reported to accommodate 16 place settings, with three racks and 360° Max Jets on the third rack. The named programs include Express Wash, Normal, ProWash, Rinse and Tough. A heating-element drying feature and 44 dBA rating are listed.

Miele G 5266 SCVi SF - an integrated option

Miele stands apart here because it is identified as a fully integrated model. The research also lists a cutlery tray, QuickIntenseWash, AutoOpen drying, ENERGY STAR qualification and a 42 dB(A) rating.

GE GDP670SYVFS - a roomy, adjustable rack layout

This GE is listed with 16 place settings, a full third rack, a caddy and an adjustable upper rack. AutoSense, Heavy, Normal, 1 Hour and Rinse are named programs, while Dry Boost offers reported drying settings. ENERGY STAR qualification and 240 kWh per year are recorded, alongside a one-year limited appliance warranty.

Café CDT875P4NW2 - drying and rack features to investigate

Café combines a reported 16-place capacity with a full third rack, adjustable upper rack and Dual Convection Ultra Dry. ENERGY STAR qualification and 240 kWh per year are also listed.

Samsung DW80CG5451SRAA - connected features and door drying

Samsung offers a reported 15-place capacity, seven programs, AutoRelease door drying, StormWash and Wi-Fi. The research also lists a third rack, a 46 dBA rating and ENERGY STAR certification at 239 kWh per year.

Maytag MDB8959SKZ - a focus on wash-zone features

Maytag’s reported feature set includes a third-level rack with wash zone, PowerBlast, Heated Dry, Dual Power Filtration and Sani Rinse. Noise is listed at 47 dBA. Its reported ten-year limited parts warranty applies to the racks, chopper blade and stainless-steel tub, not to the entire machine.

Whirlpool WDT750SAKZ - a straightforward rack-and-cycle proposition

Whirlpool is listed with three racks, 13 place settings and a 47 dBA rating. Its five named programs are Heavy, Normal, Quick Wash, Sensor Wash and Soak & Clean.

A few things to check before buying

Measure the installation, not just the cabinet. Check width, height, depth, door clearance and the precise built-in or integrated configuration of the model you intend to buy.

Think about the racks you will actually use. Look at third-rack shape, upper-rack adjustment and room for taller glasses or pans. Place-setting counts are useful, but they are not the whole story.

Separate noise ratings from real-world experience. The quoted dBA figures are supplier specifications, not sound tests we conducted. Consider where the dishwasher will run and what level of noise matters in your home.

Check drying and cycle details directly. CrystalDry, AutoOpen, heated drying and other systems are reported features, not evidence of measured superiority. Confirm the programs you use most.

Verify energy and warranty claims. ENERGY STAR status and annual energy use are important, read the exact warranty terms rather than relying on headline durations.

Our conclusion:

The Bosch SHP78CM5N is The Gable’s pick. Its reported 42 dBA noise rating, flexible third rack and CrystalDry feature make a coherent combination for shoppers interested in a quieter, adaptable dishwasher. The JennAir JDPSS246LL is the closest alternative, with an even lower listed 38 dBA figure, a reported 15-place capacity and six named wash cycles. These are research-led positions, not verified purchase recommendations.
