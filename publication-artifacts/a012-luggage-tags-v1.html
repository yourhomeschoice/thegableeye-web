THE GABLE'S LUGGAGE TAG PICK: PEAOY FOUR-PACK LUGGAGE TAGS

A luggage tag is a small purchase, but it serves an important purpose. If your suitcase goes missing or someone picks up the wrong bag, a clear way to identify its owner can make getting it back a little easier.

The best choice isn't necessarily the most expensive tag or the one with the most elaborate design. For everyday travel, we would look for three things: somewhere to write your contact details, a way to keep those details from being immediately visible, and an attachment that suits your luggage.

We compared five affordable luggage-tag options from Target and Walmart, looking at identification, privacy, attachment methods, materials and price.

Our recommendation is the Peaoy four-pack luggage tags. They offer a straightforward combination of clearly described identification fields and a flip cover over the information.

There are some important limitations to that recommendation, which we'll explain below.

Why we picked Peaoy

What stood out about the Peaoy tags was how clearly the retailer described their identification features.

Each tag is listed with a card for your name, address and contact details, protected by a flip cover and clear plastic film. The tags also have adjustable straps for attaching them to luggage.

That combination makes Peaoy a practical choice for someone who wants a conventional luggage tag with a covered information card.

The four-pack is another useful consideration if you're buying tags for several suitcases.

For our comparison, the deciding factors were the specifically described identification fields and the presence of a cover, rather than any proven performance advantage.

What matters when choosing a luggage tag?

Start with the information you want to display. Some tags have a card with clearly specified fields; others simply provide a window, insert or label. Check that the tag accommodates the contact details you actually want to use.

Consider who can see those details. A flap or cover may help keep information out of immediate view, but the presence of a cover doesn't automatically guarantee privacy. Check how it closes and what remains visible.

Look carefully at the attachment. Buckles, loops and adjustable straps all provide different ways of securing a tag. None of the product information in this comparison established which arrangement is most resistant to being pulled loose during baggage handling.

Check the pack quantity. A four-pack may be convenient for a family or several bags, while a single tag may be all you need. Compare the actual quantity you're purchasing rather than looking only at the displayed price.
